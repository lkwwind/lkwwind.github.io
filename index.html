About

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar at Vanderbilt University. My research focuses on nanophotonics, thermal photonics, metasurfaces, ultrafast laser–matter interaction, and micro/nanofabrication.

My research interests center on understanding and controlling light–matter interactions at micro- and nanoscales. I am particularly interested in developing photonic and material platforms for controlling thermal radiation, optical fields, polarization, coherence, and energy transport.

I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Clemson University, where my research focused on ultrafast laser–matter interactions and laser-induced surface structuring.

Research

Thermal Metasurfaces & Nanophotonics

Design and experimental realization of nanophotonic structures and metasurfaces for controlling thermal radiation, including spectral selectivity, directional emission, optical coherence, polarization control, and high-Q resonances.

Ultrafast Laser–Matter Interaction

Interactions of ultrashort laser pulses with materials, including transient optical responses, surface modification, ultrafast energy transfer, and laser-induced periodic surface structures.

Micro/Nanofabrication

Fabrication and characterization of micro- and nanoscale photonic structures using electron-beam lithography, photolithography, thin-film deposition, lift-off, and optical characterization.

Active & Ultrafast Photonics

Active photonic systems for dynamic control of wavelength, directionality, polarization, and coherence, with applications in sensing, imaging, communication, thermal management, and energy technologies.
