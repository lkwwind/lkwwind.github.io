About Me

I am a Postdoctoral Scholar at Vanderbilt University. My research focuses on nanophotonics, thermal photonics, metasurfaces, ultrafast laser–matter interaction, and micro/nanofabrication.

My research interests center on understanding and controlling light–matter interactions at micro- and nanoscales. I am particularly interested in the development of photonic and material platforms for controlling thermal radiation, optical fields, polarization, coherence, and energy transport.

I received my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from Clemson University, where my research focused on ultrafast laser–matter interactions and laser-induced surface structuring.

Research

Thermal Metasurfaces & Nanophotonics

My research explores the design and experimental realization of nanophotonic structures and metasurfaces for controlling thermal radiation. Of particular interest are spectral selectivity, directional emission, optical coherence, polarization control, and high-Q optical resonances.

Ultrafast Laser–Matter Interaction

I study the interaction of ultrashort laser pulses with materials, including transient optical responses, laser-induced surface modification, and ultrafast energy transfer processes. My previous work includes the formation and physical mechanisms of laser-induced periodic surface structures.

Micro/Nanofabrication

My research involves fabrication and characterization of micro- and nanoscale photonic structures using techniques including electron-beam lithography, photolithography, thin-film deposition, lift-off, and optical characterization.

Active & Ultrafast Photonics

I am interested in active photonic systems that enable dynamic control of wavelength, directionality, polarization, and coherence. These concepts have potential applications in infrared sensing, imaging, communication, thermal management, and energy technologies.
